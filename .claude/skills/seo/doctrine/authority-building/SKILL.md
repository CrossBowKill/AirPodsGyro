# Authority Building Doctrine

How a bootstrapped, low-authority site earns referring-domain authority, diversity, dofollow links,
and genuine third-party citations, usually the #1 limiter on both Google rankings and AI-engine
citation. This is the strategy layer above the `link-building-strategy`, `digital-pr-plan`, and
`review-generation-plan` workflow skills. Your domain, niche, and live pipeline state come from
`CLAUDE.local.md` and `.claude/agent-memory/seo/MEMORY.md`.

## 1. Diagnose the authority situation first

Five tells that authority (not content) is your limiter; check them against your own data:
1. You rank positions 2-9 for real commercial queries but cannot break the last few positions.
2. Google refuses to crawl sitemap'd pages ("URL unknown to Google" on deployed, sitemap-listed,
   200-serving URLs). This is the crawl-budget signature of a low-authority domain.
3. Link equity concentrated on one or two referring domains; low dofollow share; no keyword anchors.
4. Zero independent authored mentions of your brand anywhere on the public web (run a brand sweep).
5. AI engines never cite you (see geo-mastery: ~84% of AI citations flow through earned media).

When these hold, **more content is pointless**. On-page work rescues CTR on pages that already rank;
only referring-domain authority raises the ceiling. Google authority and AI citation are the same
problem wearing two hats, and this playbook is the lever for both.

## 2. The arms-not-fires discipline (the load-bearing constraint)

**The agent PREPS and TRACKS; it never executes an outward-facing action.** Every move that actually
earns a link needs a human identity, an account, a payment, a launch commitment, or a human-only
form. The division of labor is absolute:

- **The agent ARMS the operator**: drafts every directory listing, writes every community-list PR
  entry to the target repo's exact house style, specs and drafts linkable assets, writes review
  kickoff copy, drafts PR/podcast/newsletter pitches, light-verifies whether a fired move is now
  live, tracks the pipeline (drafted / submitted / pending / merged / live), and surfaces the ONE
  highest-ROI move to fire this cycle, each as GO HERE -> PASTE THIS -> gotcha.
- **The operator FIRES**: submits, opens real PRs from their identity, sends emails, posts, pays,
  creates accounts.

The agent NEVER auto-submits a directory, opens or merges a real third-party PR, sends an email,
posts to any social surface, creates an account, or makes a payment. Missing input = report the gap,
never fire blind. Why: an auto-fired off-page action is unrecoverable. A bot-submitted entry to a
quality-gated community list violates the maintainer's rules and burns the brand in the exact
community you most need; a misjudged launch post burns the one shot. The arm/fire split is what
makes aggressive authority work safe to run on an autonomous loop.

## 3. Linkable assets (the ceiling-raiser)

Directories and PRs move the FLOOR (referring-domain diversity, dofollow). **Linkable assets move
the CEILING**: they earn editorial, journalist, and .edu links passively for years, and data-driven
assets earn roughly 3x the links of opinion content [BuzzSumo]. Ranked by ROI pattern:

- **A. The annual "State of {your category}" report** [highest ceiling]. Annual State-of-X reports
  are the most-cited SaaS asset type (Stack Overflow's survey, HubSpot's State of Marketing). If
  nobody owns yours yet, it is a first-mover land-grab. The moat is data nobody else has: a
  community survey plus anonymized aggregate signal from your own product. Lean v1: one ~15-question
  survey, 150-400 responses, a tight 1,500-2,500 word report with 3-5 embeddable charts (each embed
  is a backlink) on a dedicated page. Repeatable annually; each edition keeps earning.
- **B. A free generator / calculator tool** [best effort-to-link ratio]. Free tools are the most
  durable linkable asset (a trivially simple calculator is one of Clockify's top-5 most-linked
  pages). Pick the artifact your audience's roundups would naturally link to; client-side v1, no
  backend; it doubles as an email-capture asset.
- **C. The definitive maintained category directory.** Becomes a linkable reference only if clearly
  more useful than existing community lists: categorization + honest descriptions + freshness +
  "which to use when", not a link dump. Build after A or B ships.
- **D. A free "score my X" audit mini-tool.** Markets your product's thesis; build after B proves
  the interactive-tool channel.

Sequencing: ship B first (fast, low risk), run A's survey in parallel (data collection has lead
time), then C/D. Every asset page carries a real CTA to the product.

## 4. Directories, aggregators, and community lists (the floor-raisers)

Cheap, fast, dofollow referring-domain diversity. They will not rank a head term alone, but they
break single-domain concentration and seed brand discovery. The discipline:

- **Highest-trust surface first.** If your platform vendor runs an official marketplace or catalog,
  that is the single best authority move available: a backlink plus distribution from the
  highest-trust property in your ecosystem. Follow its exact submission process (many auto-close
  direct PRs and accept only a form).
- **Then the highest-DR free dofollow set**, fastest-trust first, with the canonical description
  verbatim on each (consistency is itself a ranking + GEO signal). Flag reciprocal-link or badge
  requirements per directory; the reciprocal-vs-fee decision is the operator's.
- **Community/awesome-list mechanics**: fork, add ONE line matching the repo's exact bullet style,
  PR titled "Add {brand}" with a one-sentence body, no reformatting of neighbors, disclose
  commercial status where the entry points at your site. Match the repo's own separator style even
  when it uses a dash character you would never author, while keeping the description text you write
  clean.
- **The free-artifact-vs-paid logic (THE core decision)**: for repo-only or commercial-averse lists,
  submit your FREE focused artifact (equity lands on it and flows to your site); for lists that
  clearly accept paid products, submit the product site. Never both to one list. For any selective,
  quality-gated maintainer, lead with the free focused artifact, never the paid umbrella product.
  **The double-rejection lesson**: if a maintainer has declined you, do NOT blindly resubmit the
  same thing; find out why, change the artifact, and respect human-only submission forms
  absolutely.
- **Gated clusters**: some registry clusters require your entry to BE a specific artifact type (an
  MCP server, a plugin, an integration). Do not submit until you ship one; then fire the whole
  cluster in one coordinated push. Flag such clusters to the operator as a product-roadmap lever.

Tone rule for anything on a third-party surface: purely positive and objective, no comparative jabs
at competitors (reviewers flag them). Reserve contrast framing for your own site.

## 5. Digital PR (the editorial-link engine)

Editorial links, a journalist choosing to cite you, are the links Google and AI engines trust most.
The solo-operator 2026 standard: work several FREE expert-sourcing platforms in parallel and answer
relevant queries fast. Featured.com (the HARO successor brand), Source of Sources, Help a B2B
Writer, Qwoted, and a #journorequest saved search. The agent arms a library of reusable, on-voice
quote blocks for the recurring query types so answering is paste-and-go; the operator creates the
profiles under their real expert identity and answers 1-3 queries a day.

Podcast guesting: pitch a technical idea or a founder story, NEVER "come talk about my product".
Newsletter placements: a free genuinely useful artifact (the tool, a report data point) is a clean
pitch; the paid product alone is not. The agent drafts every pitch; the operator sends.

## 6. The review flywheel

Third-party reviews are frequently the #1 single authority lever: engines trust them far above
self-description (~88% of AI review-citations come from five platforms), and "{brand} review"
demand usually exists before any review does (check GSC).

- **Fastest-to-collect platform first** (typically Trustpilot: anyone can review in ~2 minutes),
  then the highest-B2B-weight one (typically G2: LinkedIn-verified reviewers, ~15 minutes each, so
  personally ask your most engaged customers). Target 15-25 genuine reviews.
- **The post-purchase ask**: a two-email sequence to existing buyers (day ~7-10, then a single
  follow-up to non-reviewers), personalized, from your product's transactional email identity, then
  a delayed automation for new buyers so reviews compound.
- **The non-gating rule (hard)**: invite EVERY customer to leave an HONEST review. Never incentivize
  positive reviews, never route by sentiment. If you ever thank, reward LEAVING a review, disclosed
  per platform policy.
- Once 10-20 reviews exist: a homepage testimonials section and a COMPLIANT rating via the
  platform's verified widget. Never a hard-coded aggregateRating with no visible reviews.
- Never give the paid product away free to win a review or roundup mention without the operator's
  explicit per-case decision.

## 7. The 30/60/90 sequence

Bank the cheapest high-authority wins first, light the slow fuses in parallel, save the big swing
for a real hook. "Arm" = the agent drafts; "Fire" = the operator acts.

- **Days 0-30**: fire the vendor-marketplace submission (day 1); arm+fire the highest-DR free
  directory batch; arm+fire the community-list PRs (house-style matched); stand up the free PR
  pipeline profiles; open the State-of survey (lead time); build the generator tool; reclaim any
  leaking equity (broken profile links, stale listings).
- **Days 30-60**: publish + announce the tool (newsletters, category Substacks); publish the report
  v1 with embeddable charts and pitch it the week it drops; first podcast pitches; second directory
  wave and wave-1 resubmits.
- **Days 60-90**: the launch post (Show HN or equivalent) timed to the strongest hook, the tool or
  the report, never the bare paid product; the coordinated gated-cluster push if the qualifying
  artifact shipped; a guest editorial off the report's data; monthly backlink re-pull and recur.

## 8. Measurement

- **Primary KPI**: referring-domain count + diversity + dofollow share, and single-domain
  concentration falling. Monthly pull from a real backlink tool.
- **The honest DR ceiling**: for a bootstrapped product, roughly 45-55, not 90. Track movement
  toward it; do not chase a number the domain age cannot support.
- **Pipeline status is the leading indicator**: drafted -> submitted -> pending -> merged/live.
  Live entries move before DR does.
- **The crawl signal is the real-world confirmation**: the day Google starts crawling your
  previously ignored sitemap'd pages is the day authority crossed the threshold.
- **Never claim an authority win from a drafted-but-unfired asset.** Only a fired-and-live move
  counts. Report truthfully: what was drafted, what the operator fired, what is now live.

## Hard rules

- ARMS, never FIRES. No auto-submission, no real third-party PRs, no emails, no posts, no payments.
- One canonical brand description everywhere, verbatim.
- Reviews non-gated, never incentivized-for-positive.
- Never fabricate a backlink, metric, pipeline status, or review.
- Via negativa: directories are not the growth lever (assets + reviews + presence are); more blog
  content is not an authority move; never force a launch without a hook; skip archived or closed
  targets permanently once verified.
