# Ad Hook Library

## Purpose

The first second decides the ad. 70-80% of viewing is muted, hook rate is the primary early metric, and hook fatigue decays a creative in weeks. This skill gives the scriptwriter and strategist the hook families that work for a skeptical technical audience in 2026, the mechanics behind each, templates with the specifics left as slots, and the benchmarks to predict against. No real person's name is ever a template; borrowed authority is always a widely reported habit or role, never an endorsement.

**Category**: Copywriting / Scriptwriting

## Inputs

- **The brief**: format, angle, the substance beat (what the hook may promise), the hypothesis (which variable the hook tests)
- **The brand memory**: banned words, voice calibration pairs, the product's TRUE capabilities
- **The pain list** for your audience (ranked, from research): the hook mirrors the top pains in the viewer's own words

## System Context

Read `video-ads/foundations/PLAYBOOK.md` (hook family values), `MISTAKES-LOG.md` M-A02 / M-A03 / M-A05 / M-A11 (what fails a hook), and `RECIPE.md` section 1d (the 3s hook holds max 7-8 spoken words).

## The 2026 benchmarks (predict against these)

- Hook rate (3-second views / impressions): 28% on Meta, 33% on TikTok; 30-35% is strong. It stabilises 24-48h after launch.
- Hold rate: 20-25% is strong.
- The hook lands by 1.0s on Reels; the pain is in text within 1s; the mechanism by 10s; the CTA at 22-25s.
- Negative framing lifts hook rate 1.3-1.8x over positive framing.
- Hook-type hit rates (2026 creative benchmarks): newness 11.4%, price anchor 10.9%, confession 8.7%. Style hit rates: text-only 11.6% > UGC 7.6% > high-production 7.0%; letter format 10.8%, unconventional text placement 9.6%, founder 8.6%, ASMR 8.6%, demo 8.1%.
- Stat-led openers dominate technical B2B. Proof over people for a low-trust audience (around 29% of developers say they trust AI tool output).

## The hook families

Every template: on-screen version 5-8 words burned on frame 1; spoken version up to 7-8 words in 3s; specific, true, promising only what the substance beat shows.

### 1. Pain-mirror
**Mechanic:** the viewer's exact pain in their own words, so they nod before they think. Highest frequency x intensity family in the 2026 research sweep.
**Templates:** "Every new session is a blank slate. You re-explain again." / "It said done. It wasn't." / "You bought an AI agent and became its supervisor." / "I'm babysitting {tool} too much."
**Pairs with:** the belief shift ("{tool} isn't dumb. Your missing system is.") then a numbers proof.

### 2. Shock-fact
**Mechanic:** a real, sourced, counter-intuitive number burned on frame 1 over a slow object render; the cut to the proof resolves it. The most duplicable family (swap the stat, keep the skeleton).
**Templates:** "{N}% of your tokens go to re-reading history." / "{N}% of {role} spend more time fixing AI output than writing." / "A ${N} plan, gone in {N} minutes." / "{N}% of the code a leading AI lab ships is written by its own model."
**Rule:** the number is public, sourced in the brief, and current. A stat you cannot cite is a false claim (M-A01).

### 3. Authority (habit, never endorsement)
**Mechanic:** borrow trust from a widely reported HABIT of a trusted group or role, open a curiosity gap (what is it?), reveal it is a habit anyone can copy, position the product as the fastest way to adopt it. Trust is borrowed from the habit, never from a claimed endorsement; the product is the tool for the habit, not the authority's tool. Defensible on ad platforms (no implied endorsement), honest (no fabricated usage claim), high-curiosity (a retention engine).
**Templates:** "The setup ritual the best engineers run before they touch {tool}." / "The folder every top team's {tool} has, and yours doesn't." / "Report: the configuration habit separating top builders from everyone else." / "What if you pasted the same prompt into the {tool} of the people who BUILT it?"
**Rails:** never depict a named person's likeness or voice; never name a real person in a spoken line (moderation reject + M-A05); if a widely reported figure must be referenced, it lives in the burned text card as a reported habit, and the spoken line uses an epithet. Never state anyone uses your product unless they have said so publicly and gave permission.

### 4. Contrarian
**Mechanic:** flatly deny the consensus, then earn it with one reason and one stat. Argument, not enthusiasm.
**Templates:** "Prompt engineering doesn't matter. Verification does." / "Vibe coding is over." / "You don't want a co-pilot. You want an operator." / "Prompt packs are dead."

### 5. Confession
**Mechanic:** a first-person admission in the skeptic register (self-aware, transparent, skeptic-in-charge), the one ad voice a technical audience tolerates. Objection first, surprise second, limitation admitted out loud.
**Templates:** "AI coding hype is mostly BS. I say that as someone who uses it daily." / "Day one, I was not impressed." / "I cancelled it. Then this." / "I was babysitting {tool} for hours a day."
**Rule:** never a ring-light testimonial ("changed my life", "obsessed", "you NEED this"): dead openers, M-A02.

### 6. Challenge
**Mechanic:** a dare or a comparison the viewer wants to watch resolved, muted. Best with a split screen.
**Templates:** "Same prompt. Two {tool} setups. Watch." / "Fix one bad diff, live, no cuts." / "I ran it overnight. Here's what shipped."

### 7. Numbers (personal measurement)
**Mechanic:** a specific, measured, personal before/after with units and a duration. Reads as evidence.
**Templates:** "40 tabs down to 3, measured for a week." / "{N} re-explanations a day to zero." / "{N} sessions, {N} corrections, one rule file."

### 8. Belief-shift
**Mechanic:** reframe the pain as a missing-systems problem, never model-blame or user-blame. The top 2026 structure family; usually beat 3, but works as a cold open on text.
**Templates:** "{tool} isn't dumb. You just don't have the right systems." / "Same model. Same prompt. Different results. Because of what's around it."

### Also valid (UGC family)
disconfirmed-expectation ("I thought this was hype. I was wrong, here's the specific thing."), honest-limitation ("It won't write your architecture. It WILL run every repetitive task."), category-reframe, insider-decode ("the config 99% of users never set up"), peer-authority ("12 years shipping to prod; first tool in a decade I told my team to install", as a role, never a name), problem-recognition, stop-doing-X ("Stop babysitting your AI.").

### Dead and saturated (refuse)
Ring-light testimonials; cold rhetorical questions ("Wondering...", "Have you ever...", "Imagine..." as the first words); "anti-hype / no-BS" as the whole pitch; generic "AI is changing everything"; a hook with no payoff (throttled post-retention-update); "POV:" as a wrapper more than once a month.

## Process

1. Pick the family the brief's hypothesis names. Write the on-screen version first (5-8 words), then the spoken version (<= 8 words).
2. Confirm it promises only what the substance beat shows, names no real person in speech, uses no hype word, is not a dead opener.
3. Write 8-10 variants across >= 3 families over the SAME body. Number them. This is the A/B set: hold the proof, vary the hook.
4. Predict: pick the metric (hook rate) and the baseline (matched n >= 3, else 28% / 33%); the negative-framing and stat-led priors shift the prediction up modestly, never beyond +20% without data.

## Output Format

```
Hook set: {id}
Primary ({family}): on-screen "{5-8 words}" / spoken "{<= 8 words}"
Variants (8-10, families {a, b, c}):
 1. ...
Prediction: hook_rate {x}% vs baseline {y}% ({matched n | external prior})
Checks: M-A02 pass, M-A03 pass, M-A05 pass, silent-legible yes, promise true yes
```

## Best Practices

- Front-load credibility, never enthusiasm. A number, a pain, or a contrarian claim in the first second.
- Negative framing wins hook rate; honesty wins the click. Pair them.
- The hook foreshadows the payoff; the body must deliver it or the platform throttles the ad.
- Rotate families across a batch; two ads testing the same family on the same format teach nothing.
